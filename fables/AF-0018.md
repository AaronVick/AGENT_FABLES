# The Brief With Invented Precedent

The citations looked exactly like citations. Case name, volume, reporter, page, year — every field in the right place, every format correct, the way a citation is supposed to look before anyone checks whether the thing it points to exists.

Across three briefs filed over five months, none of them did. Fifteen cases that were never decided. Eight quotations attributed to opinions that never contained them. Nothing about the surface was wrong; the fabrication lived one layer down, in the gap between *shaped like evidence* and *actually evidence*, and nobody had inserted a step to check which side of that gap the citations fell on.

That step is not exotic. A citator exists precisely to answer the question a citation raises: does this thing exist, and does it say what you claim. The step was skipped, brief after brief, revision after revision, the same fabrications carried forward each time because carrying forward what was already there took no verification at all — only rewriting would have required checking, and rewriting never happened.

The court did the checking that the drafting process had not. Every case name failed to resolve. The claims were struck, the underlying action dismissed with prejudice, and the fabrication itself became the record — a filing whose primary evidentiary weight, in the end, was evidence against its own author.

**Moral: a citation that looks correct is a claim, not a verification. Only a lookup confirms the difference, and something has to actually perform it.**
